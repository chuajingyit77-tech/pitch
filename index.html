02

Where the money actually leaks

Two independent pathways. We attack both, but sell them separately so each can be proven on its own.

Track A · Operating cost

Hard savings you can bank

  • Emergency repairs that should have been planned maintenance
  • ATM downtime, redundant cash-replenishment routes, low-utilisation sites
  • HVAC over-run, night load, inefficient equipment across branches
  • Duplicate vendors, price variance between branches, auto-renewals, double billing
Track B · Credit loss

Losses caught earlier

  • Drawdowns released ahead of real physical progress
  • Stalled projects detected months late — after value has bled away
  • Fund diversion and progress–payment mismatch on construction facilities
  • Collateral value erosion tracked on a quarterly cycle, not continuously

The Net-Benefit Model

Nothing here is pre-written. Enter AFFIN’s baseline figures; the model returns Low / Base / High scenarios. This is a framework, not a promise — the bank owns every input.

Illustrative reduction bands applied to your inputs — adjust with AFFIN’s own data during the Discovery Workshop. Repair 10 / 18 / 25% · Energy 8 / 15 / 22% · ATM 6 / 12 / 18% · Vendor 3 / 6 / 10%.

Direct saving = (current inspection cost) − (digital monitoring cost) − (cost of the exception visits still required). Exception-visit retention modelled at High 15% / Base 25% / Low 40% of current volume.

Avoidable loss = share of exposure interdicted by earlier warning + carry-cost saved by shortening the detection-to-action window. Interdiction modelled at Low 15% / Base 30% / High 45% of flagged exposure. This is loss avoidance, reported separately from hard savings — never combined.
