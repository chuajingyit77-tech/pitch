# Section 9 — Investment Model

> **Status:** Proposed structures for discussion. No investor commitments exist. All structures are subject to the due diligence outcomes (Section 11) and confirmation of land/rights by the project owner.

## 9.1 Potential investor universe

| Category | Examples of investor types | What they need to see |
|----------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| Strategic — construction/infra | Shandong Hi-Speed Group and peers | EPC pipeline + equity upside, government endorsement |
| Strategic — logistics operators | Port/terminal operators, rail logistics groups, 3PLs | Operating control or strong operating agreements, cargo commitments |
| Logistics real estate platforms | Regional industrial/logistics developers and funds | Leasable warehouse product, tenant covenants, yield profile |
| Financial — infrastructure funds | ASEAN/Asia infrastructure and private equity funds | Contracted revenue share, credible exit, governance rights |
| Malaysian institutional investors | GLICs / pension-type institutions (subject to their mandates) | National-interest alignment, stable yield, local partner quality |
| Development finance / policy lenders | Multilateral and bilateral DFIs, export credit agencies, policy banks | ESG compliance, trade-facilitation development impact, sovereign context |
| State/federal vehicles | State economic development corporations, federal facilitation funds | Jobs, state revenue, border modernisation outcomes |

## 9.2 Revenue streams (categories only — quantification pending feasibility)

**Core terminal:** rail terminal handling, container lift/storage, transshipment fees, reefer plugs, empty depot.
**Warehousing:** ambient/bonded/cold-chain leasing, VAS (kitting, labelling, palletising), e-fulfilment.
**Border services:** truck marshalling/queue management fees, weighbridge, inspection support services to agencies, paid truck parking.
**Commercial:** office/retail rents, fuel/service concessions, advertising.
**Digital:** VBS/booking fees, data products, reefer monitoring services, portal transaction fees.
**Development/management:** PMO fees, asset management fees, expansion development fees (these accrue to service providers rather than the SPV).

## 9.3 Capital structure (indicative logic, no figures)

- **Senior debt:** project finance term debt sized against contracted/quasi-contracted revenue; potential lenders include Malaysian commercial banks, regional banks, ECAs supporting the EPC's country of origin, and multilaterals. Typical infra gearing ranges are established in due diligence and the financial model — **not asserted here**.
- **Mezzanine/subordinated:** optional layer for gap funding, potentially from strategic sponsors.
- **Equity:** sponsor equity from the consortium (Section 13); staged drawdown against milestones.
- **Government support (to be explored, not assumed):** land tenure arrangements, tax incentives (e.g., promoted-activity incentives via MIDA, free-zone status), viability gap or facilitation funding where programmes exist. All subject to government engagement — **pending confirmation**.

## 9.4 Equity structure principles

- Control aligned with accountability: parties bearing delivery/operating risk hold governance rights matching their exposure.
- Local participation: meaningful Malaysian shareholding supports approvals, land matters and long-term political durability; state participation (equity or land-for-equity) is an option to be explored with the state government — **pending confirmation**.
- Reserved matters, pre-emption rights, and deadlock resolution defined in the shareholders' agreement at consortium formation (Phase 2 of roadmap).

## 9.5 PPP model options

| Model | Description | When it fits |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| Private land, private build-own-operate | SPV acquires/leases private land, builds and operates as a licensed private facility | If land is privately held and no state concession is required |
| State land lease + BOT/BOO | Long lease of state land to SPV; build-operate-transfer or build-own-operate | If land is state-owned; requires state agreement |
| Concession with government facilities | SPV finances/builds agency facilities (WP11) against concession rights or availability-style arrangements | If government wants integrated border facilities |
| Hybrid landlord model | SPV as landlord developer; specialist operators (terminal, cold chain) as tenants/operators | Reduces SPV operating risk; multiplies partner slots |

The applicable model depends entirely on the true land and rights position — **pending confirmation from project owner**.

## 9.6 Exit options

- Trade sale to strategic operator or logistics real estate platform
- Sale to infrastructure fund once cash flows season
- REIT injection of stabilised warehouse assets (Malaysian industrial REITs are active acquirers)
- IPO of the platform company at scale
- Refinancing/recapitalisation for partial sponsor liquidity

## 9.7 Risk allocation principles

- Construction risk → EPC contractor via fixed-price date-certain wrap with LDs
- Cargo/market risk → shared: anchor commitments + sponsor equity buffer
- Operating performance → operator via KPI-linked O&M contract
- Border policy/customs cooperation → mitigated via government engagement, not transferable
- Land/approvals → precondition to financial close; carried by development phase, not by lenders

## 9.8 Funding stages

1. **Development capital** (sponsors; highest risk) — feasibility, DD, approvals, consortium formation
2. **Financial close** — equity + debt commitments against bankable package
3. **Construction drawdowns** — milestone-based, monitored by lenders' engineer
4. **Operations ramp** — working capital lines; possible capex holdback releases
5. **Expansion funding** — Phase 2+ from retained cash, debt headroom, or new partners
