# Section 2 — Public Research (Verified Sources Only)

> Every statement below carries a reference [R#] resolved in `17_references.md`. Research date: 26 July 2026. Where sources conflict, the conflict is stated. Items that could not be verified are listed at the end — they are **excluded** from all business argumentation in this package.

## 2.1 Padang Besar — the border crossing

- Padang Besar is a twin border town: Malaysian side in Perlis, Thai side in Sadao District, Songkhla Province [R1, R2].
- It is both a road crossing (ICQS complex) and **Malaysia's only direct rail link with Thailand** [R2, R3].
- The railway station (opened 1918) hosts **juxtaposed border controls** — Malaysian and Thai immigration/customs/quarantine co-located inside the station [R3].
- Road border operating hours are approximately 06:00–22:00 Malaysia time — not a 24-hour crossing [R4, R5]. *Current hours to be re-verified in due diligence.*

## 2.2 Rail connectivity and rail cargo

- KTMB's electrified, double-tracked West Coast Line (metre gauge, ETS services up to 140 km/h) terminates at Padang Besar; SRT's Southern Line continues to Hat Yai and Bangkok [R3, R29].
- **Both networks are metre gauge — no break of gauge at Padang Besar.** The standard-gauge break for China traffic is at Thanaleng (Laos) on the China–Laos Railway [R9, R30].
- Freight services through the crossing:
  - KTMB Kargo landbridge services (target ~800 TEU/month scope across Penang Port, Port Klang, Padang Besar; 4-day transit concept) [R10]
  - CMA CGM / Infinity **Penang–Padang Besar block train** (from Aug–Sep 2022), corridor reported up to 7,500 TEU/month [R11]
  - **ASEAN Express** (launched 27 June 2024): Kontena Nasional ICD (Kelana Jaya) → Padang Besar → Lat Krabang ICD → Thanaleng Dry Port → Chongqing; ~9–14 days vs 14–21 by sea; initially ~2 round trips/week [R12, R31]
  - **STC Perai–Perlis Inland Port daily freight service** (first train, 80 TEU, 1 December 2025) [R13]
- The legacy Padang Besar Container Terminal (TKPB): capacity 150,000 TEU/yr; 2024 volumes grew ~21% YoY to ~80% of capacity [R15, R32].
- 13MP-era announcements (Oct 2025) include a Padang Besar Depot upgrade, a national "Road-to-Rail" cargo shift initiative, and North Butterworth Container Terminal upgrades [R33]. KTMB road-to-rail volumes rose 23.5% YoY in January 2026 [R34].
- Thai side: SRT southern double-tracking packages were slated for bidding within 2026, including **Hat Yai–Padang Besar (45 km, ~7.77bn baht)** — explicitly strategic for the Malaysia corridor [R35].

## 2.3 Perlis Inland Port (PIP) — the operating incumbent

- Developer/operator: Perlis Inland Port Sdn Bhd, wholly-owned by **Mutiara Perlis Sdn Bhd** — an 80:20 JV of Mutiara Infra Sdn Bhd and the Perlis state government via Menteri Besar Inc [R14, R22].
- PPP delivery model: **NCIA funded external infrastructure** (~5 km bonded road, ~1 km rail spur, flyover; government spend reported >RM300m, with figures of ~RM400m for external works and a RM327m federal allocation reported in July 2026); the port itself built by private investors [R23, R24, R26, R36].
- Financing: Bank Pembangunan Malaysia approved **RM373.54m** [R25]. Phase 1 gross development cost reported RM450.05m + RM41.35m financing costs [R37].
- Site: **500 acres / ~202.3 ha** in the Chuping Valley–Padang Besar area, with dedicated bonded road to the border and direct rail siding to the double-track network [R17, R23].
- Timeline: construction from 2022; first commercial freight service 1 December 2025; operations migrated from TKPB on 12 April 2026; the **Tuanku Syed Sirajuddin Free Commercial Zone at PIP was launched by the Raja of Perlis on 30 May 2026** [R13, R16, R17, R28].
- Capacity: Phase 1 stated at 300,000 TEU/yr; current capability stated at **600,000 TEU/yr** (official, May 2026); ultimate capacity reported at 1M and 2M TEU/yr in different sources — **conflicting, unconfirmed** [R17, R27, R38].
- KTMB signed an **O&M agreement** for PIP (March/April 2025) [R16].
- Commercial ecosystem: MOC with **Maersk Logistics & Services Malaysia**; MOU with Bina Darulaman; RM17m Kalmar handling equipment order; Citaglobal acquired 12.8% of Mutiara Perlis (Aug 2025); Berjaya Property invested RM58m in a related developer entity (Jul 2026) [R22, R39, R40, R41].
- Positioning: promoted as a node linking ASEAN with China/Central Asia/Europe via the Pan-Asian rail network; part of an aspirational "Perlis Maritime Corridor" concept (PIP + proposed Sanglang Port + Perlis Power Hub, reported ~US$6.5bn GDC — **aspirational master plan, not committed**) [R17, R40].

## 2.4 Road cargo and road infrastructure

- Federal Route 7 begins at the border (Kilometre Zero) toward Alor Setar; Federal Route 79 (28.5 km) links Padang Besar to Ulu Pauh; expressway access is via the Changlun corridor [R6, R7].
- The **Bukit Kayu Hitam–Sadao corridor (~45 km east) is the busiest Malaysia–Thailand road crossing**; a new ICQS–CIQ connecting road was jointly launched by both Prime Ministers on 10 July 2026 (11 vehicle lanes + 14 immigration lanes per side; cargo terminal with dual fast-scan X-ray) [R8, R42].
- Padang Besar ICQS handles commercial cargo vehicles; border hours constrain trucking (Section 2.1) [R43].

## 2.5 Free Commercial Zone and customs

- The Tuanku Syed Sirajuddin Free Commercial Zone at PIP: 500-acre FCZ with bonded road and rail connectivity (launched 30 May 2026) [R17]. Formal gazettement details under the Free Zones Act 1990 were **not found publicly**.
- Malaysian customs (JKDM/RMCD) administers the land entry point; national digitalisation runs through uCustoms/SMK and the **ASEAN Single Window** (full e-Form D exchange live across all 10 AMS; ACDD exchange as of 2025) [R18, R19, R44].
- ASEAN transit facilitation: AFAFGIT (1998/2000) implemented via the ASEAN Customs Transit System (ACTS), piloted by Singapore–Malaysia–Thailand; AFAFIST (2009) covers inter-state transport [R45].
- A Malaysia–Thailand single-stop inspection concept for this border has been studied (CAREC/ADB paper); implementation status unverified [R21].

## 2.6 Malaysia–Thailand trade

- Bilateral trade: averaged US$24.7bn/yr (2017–2023); US$27.7bn peak (2022); **US$25.06bn in 2024, with land-border trade ~40% of the total** [R46, R47].
- Official target: **US$30bn by 2027**, agreed at the 3rd Malaysia–Thailand Joint Trade Committee, with a joint task force on trade facilitation and connectivity [R46].
- Thai customs data (2024): Padang Besar customs house handled **102.3bn baht (+25.9% YoY)** — 3rd nationally in transshipment trade value; Sadao handled 212.6bn baht. **Padang Besar is the fastest-growing of the two corridors** [R48].
- Thai-side border trade with Malaysia, 2025 full year: 315.1bn baht (+2.8%) — Thailand's largest border-trade partner [R49].
- Leading flows: HDDs and processed wood (exports via Sadao/Padang Besar), rubber (TSNR via Songkhla checkpoints +39.3%); F&B and E&E cited as top cross-border categories [R48, R50].
- ~70% of southern Thailand's export cargo is transshipped through Penang (Penang Port Commission chairman, April 2026) — the landbridge rationale [R51].

## 2.7 ASEAN and corridor context

- MPAC 2025 (seamless logistics as a strategic area) concluded; successor **ASEAN Connectivity Strategic Plan 2026–2035** operational from 2026 [R52].
- IMT-GT (Indonesia–Malaysia–Thailand Growth Triangle) covers Perlis and Songkhla; PIP is framed as strengthening Perlis' IMT-GT role [R53].
- Kunming–Singapore railway context: China–Laos Railway operating since Dec 2021 (standard gauge); Thailand's standard-gauge Bangkok–Nakhon Ratchasima line ~36% complete (target 2028), Phase 2 to Nong Khai target 2031 [R30, R54].
- Thai Land Bridge (Chumphon–Ranong) remains in study/approval flux (fresh EHIA ordered June 2026; Phase 1 target 2030) — a potential long-run change to corridor economics, not a current constraint [R55].

## 2.8 Northern Corridor, NCIA and federal initiatives

- NCIA: statutory body (Act 687, est. 2008) for the NCER (Perlis, Kedah, Penang, Perak); logistics is a mandated sector [R56].
- NCIA delivered PIP's external infrastructure and positions PIP as anchor for the **Chuping Valley Industrial Area (CVIA — 2,482 acres, green/halal industries)** and NCER AgriBio Economic Zone [R23, R57].
- NCIA projections (2022): PIP to contribute RM2.8bn to state GDP and 500 high-value jobs by 2030, expanding border trade via Padang Besar by 50% [R24].
- Federal frame: National Transport Policy 2019–2030; Logistics and Trade Facilitation Masterplan (2015–2020, successor not published); NIMP 2030 treats logistics as an enabler [R58, R59].
- **Malaysia–Thailand Border Economic Zone**: announced by PM Anwar (10 July 2026) spanning Perlis, Kedah, Kelantan, Perak, Penang — boundaries and incentives not yet published [R60].
- PPP policy: **PIKAS 2030** (launched 9 Sep 2024): RM78bn private investment target, competitive RFP bidding preference, "user-pays" priority; UKAS under the Prime Minister's Department remains the PPP gatekeeper [R61].
- Foreign ownership: ports/strategic concessions require **≥51% Malaysian ownership** (reaffirmed by the Transport Minister, 17 July 2026); logistics licensing carries Malaysian/Bumiputera equity conditions by sub-sector [R62, R63].

## 2.9 Perlis state context

- MB of Perlis led an investment drive (MIDA Invest Series, Feb 2025): Mutiara Perlis MOU/MOC exchanges (Bina Darulaman; Maersk), ~RM800m expected economic gains cited [R39].
- Perlis 2026 state budget: RM359.58m — the state's largest ever [R64].
- Padang Besar is a designated state growth area [R65].

## 2.10 Shandong Hi-Speed Group (SDHS) — verified profile

- Shandong provincial SOE, HQ Jinan; transport infrastructure core; operates >9,000 km of expressways; businesses across China and abroad [R66, R67].
- Scale (company-published, needs bond-prospectus cross-check): 2025 revenue ~RMB 295bn; total assets ~RMB 1.76tn; Fortune Global 500 (401st) [R67].
- Rail: major shareholder/builder of the Jinan–Qingdao HSR (~US$9.1bn); operates the **"Qilu" China–Europe freight trains** (2,500 Shandong-handled trips in 2023; routes to Central Asia; China–Vietnam extension) [R68, R69].
- EPC arm: **Shandong Hi-Speed Road & Bridge Group** (000498.SZ, revenue ~RMB 70bn LTM) with international EPC track record [R70].
- **China Shandong International (CSI)** — wholly-owned since 2008 — has a Malaysian-registered entity [R71].
- **Verified Malaysia activity:** framework agreement with Bursa-listed **PTT Synergy Group (23 Sep 2025)** for smart/automated warehousing — Malaysian media reported ~RM2bn scope; Chinese trade press reported RM6bn/2 million pallet positions — **scope discrepancy unresolved** [R72, R73].
- Ratings: Moody's first-time A3 (parent, Feb 2018 — current rating not publicly confirmable); Fitch A－/Stable on HK-listed Shandong Hi-Speed Holdings [R74].
- **No published SDHS commitment to Perlis or Padang Besar exists.** Any SDHS role in this project is proposal-stage only.

## 2.11 Benchmarks and lessons

- **Thanaleng Dry Port / Vientiane Logistics Park (Laos)** — closest structural analogue: 50-year PPP concession (2020) to a private SPV; 382 ha; ~US$727m program; IFC-arranged US$67m package + AIIB US$10m; gauge-break transshipment yard (2022); partners include Kerry Logistics and COSCO [R75, R76].
- **Lat Krabang ICD (Thailand)**: SRT as concession administrator, six modules leased ~10 years to private operators; expanded from 400k to 1.7M TEU [R77].
- **Malaysian dry ports** (Ipoh 1989, Nilai 1995, Segamat 1998): published lessons — metre gauge precludes double-stacking, track capacity limits, single-mode dependence, weak seaport recognition [R78].
- **Port Klang Free Zone (cautionary)**: cost escalation RM2bn→RM7.85bn, governance failures documented by PwC review — the discipline argument for evidence-first development [R79].
- Financing landscape: AIIB signed agreements with Maybank, CIMB, AmBank and BPMB (Oct 2025) to mobilise up to **US$6bn** for sustainable ASEAN infrastructure incl. sukuk structures; BPMB is the proven lender to this exact asset class (PIP); Sime Darby Property × LOGOS raised RM1bn for industrial development (PNB, KWAP, Great Eastern anchor investors) [R80, R81, R82].
