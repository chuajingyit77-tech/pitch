A physical verification layer
for the credit book

Prepared for
AFFIN Bank Berhad

Commercial in confidence

A bank can see every ringgit it has released. It cannot see the building that ringgit was supposed to become. This proposal closes that gap — continuously, at portfolio scale, without a site visit — and then points the same infrastructure at the bank's own property costs.

The problem in one sentence: on a typical construction facility, money is released against a progress claim that nobody independently measures. Drawdown runs ahead of the structure that secures it, and the bank finds out when the borrower stops paying.

What is missing today

8%
Typical share of the secured book physically inspected in a given year — the rest is sampled or self-reported.
3–5 days
Turnaround for a single valuation site visit, at RM 3,000–5,000 per instruction.
T+1 quarter
Typical lag between a physical change on a site and the credit file reflecting it.

What we propose to build

One platform, thirteen modules, delivered in phases. Group B modules protect the credit book. Group A modules take cost out of the bank's own premises using the same drones, sensors, GIS and computer-vision infrastructure — which is why the operating savings can fund the risk platform rather than competing with it for budget.

The single example worth remembering

A RM 240 m development facility. Cumulative drawdown stands at 61.7%. An aerial survey registered against the BIM model measures the structure at 47.3% complete, to ±1.8 percentage points. The bank has advanced RM 34.6 m against work that does not yet exist. The gap opened six months earlier and widened every month — invisible in every report the bank currently receives, and unambiguous the moment it is measured.

Detecting one such position a year, on one facility, pays for the entire programme.

How it starts — a 90-day pilot, not a platform purchase

WEEK 1 – 4 · DISCOVERY

Prove the numbers

Workshops with Credit Risk, Property and IT Security. The savings model is re-based on the bank's actuals. The RMiT third-party assessment pack is submitted before any integration begins.

Gate: a baseline both sides have signed.

WEEK 5 – 9 · LIVE PILOT

One district, one portfolio

Eight construction facilities under B1 monitoring; twelve branches instrumented for A1, A2 and A5. Read-only integration — no change to core banking.

Gate: at least one finding the bank did not already have.

WEEK 10 – 13 · VALIDATION

Measured, then decided

Results against the week-4 baseline. Audit and Compliance walk the evidence trail. Rollout plan, total cost of ownership and exit plan tabled together.

Gate: the bank decides. No lock-in; the data remains the bank's.

Why this is deliverable in Malaysia

Regulatory and data

  • Deployed in-country. The bank retains ownership and control of all data and cryptographic keys, consistent with BNM's Risk Management in Technology policy for cloud and third-party arrangements.
  • Third-party due diligence, business continuity and a documented exit plan are submitted in week 1, ahead of integration work.
  • No personal data leaves the bank's environment; site imagery is redacted for faces and vehicle plates at the point of capture, consistent with the PDPA 2010.

Operations and law

  • Flights are conducted by a Malaysian operator holding CAAM ATF permits with RCoC-B rated pilots and JUPEM approval for aerial data capture. Permits are filed at least 14 working days ahead and tracked in the platform.
  • Digital signing is issued through a Malaysian licensed Certification Authority under the Digital Signature Act 1997 — not a foreign CA.
  • Implementation and first-line support are delivered by a Malaysian entity, with source escrow and documented handover.

Who delivers it

The technology is supplied by a consortium of three Chinese technology groups — two publicly listed and one state-owned — with a combined track record in intelligent transport, digital twin and large-scale infrastructure monitoring. The lead technology partner is a Beijing Stock Exchange listed company (873806) with 28 years in the field, more than 400 registered intellectual property rights, and deployments across 30 provinces. Implementation in Malaysia is through a local delivery entity.

Full corporate profiles, audited financials, reference project dossiers and client references are available on request and are provided in full as part of the vendor due-diligence pack.
