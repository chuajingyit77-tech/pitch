# Section 3 — Current Ecosystem at Padang Besar

> Facts below are drawn from the cited research in Section 2 / References. Diagram descriptions are provided for the deck; verified facts vs. interpretation are flagged.

## 3.1 The border system (verified structure)

Padang Besar is a twin town split by the Malaysia–Thailand frontier (Perlis, Malaysia / Sadao District, Songkhla, Thailand). It is Malaysia's **only direct rail link with Thailand**, and hosts both a road ICQS complex and a railway station with **juxtaposed Malaysian and Thai border controls inside the station**. Border road hours are approximately 06:00–22:00 (not 24-hour), a structural throughput constraint. [References R1–R5]

## 3.3 Element-by-element

**Road.** Federal Route 7 starts at the border (Kilometre Zero) toward Alor Setar; FT79 links Padang Besar to Ulu Pauh; expressway access via the Changlun corridor. The competing road gateway is Bukit Kayu Hitam–Sadao (~45 km east), where a new ICQS–CIQ connecting road was launched in July 2026 — road cargo growth may increasingly favour that corridor. [R6–R8]

**Rail.** KTMB's electrified double-track West Coast Line terminates at Padang Besar and connects to SRT's Southern Line. Both networks are metre gauge — **no gauge break at this border**; the standard-gauge break for China traffic is in Laos (Thanaleng). Freight: KTMB landbridge services, the CMA CGM/Infinity Penang–Padang Besar block train (from 2022), the ASEAN Express (Malaysia–Thailand–Laos–China, from June 2024), and the daily Perai–PIP "STC" service (from December 2025). [R9–R13]

**Warehouse.** Historic capacity around Padang Besar town is limited; PIP introduces new warehousing targeting halal, automotive and rubber sectors. Broader northern-region warehousing sits in Penang/Kedah. [R14]

**Container.** The legacy Padang Besar Container Terminal (TKPB) has ~150,000 TEU/yr capacity and was running at ~80% before operations migrated to PIP (migration completed April 2026). PIP's stated current capability is 600,000 TEU/yr. [R15–R17]

**Cold chain.** No large-scale multi-user cold-chain facility at the border is documented in public sources — an evident gap given perishables flows (Thai fruit/seafood south, Malaysian halal north). *Interpretation, to be validated in feasibility.*

**Border & Customs.** Malaysian side: JKDM/RMCD at the ICQS and station; rail benefits from juxtaposed controls. Digital customs: uCustoms/ASEAN Single Window progression is documented nationally, but live status at Padang Besar specifically is unverified. Thai side: CIQ upgrade plans reported (single source). [R18–R21]

**The incumbent: Perlis Inland Port.** 500 acres at the Chuping Valley/Padang Besar area; developed by Perlis Inland Port Sdn Bhd under Mutiara Perlis (80:20 Mutiara Infra / MB Inc Perlis); NCIA-funded external works (bonded road ~5 km, ~1 km rail spur, flyover, government spend >RM300m reported); BPMB financing RM373.54m approved; KTMB O&M agreement (2025); first freight service December 2025; the Tuanku Syed Sirajuddin Free Commercial Zone launched 30 May 2026. **Any new platform must define its relationship to PIP — integrate, complement, or compete — before approaching investors.** [R22–R28]

## 3.4 Ecosystem gaps observed (team interpretation — for validation)

1. Cold chain at scale at the border — not documented; likely white space
2. Truck marshalling/queue management for the 06:00–22:00 window — congestion mechanics documented at TKPB
3. Digital corridor visibility (booking, ANPR, clearance status) — no integrated system publicly documented
4. VAS/light processing inside a free zone adjacent to the border — early stage at PIP
5. 24-hour border operations — a bilateral policy matter, repeatedly raised publicly, unresolved
