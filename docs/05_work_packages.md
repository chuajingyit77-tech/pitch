# Section 5 — EPC Work Package Breakdown (WP01–WP20)

> **Status:** Proposed structure by the development team. Scope, siting, land area and phasing are **pending confirmation from project owner**. No work package below implies that land, approvals or concession rights currently exist.

The proposed Padang Besar Cross-Border Logistics Platform is broken into twenty EPC work packages so that (a) the consortium can allocate scope between Shandong Hi-Speed Group entities and local contractors, (b) financing can be staged against discrete completion milestones, and (c) early-revenue packages can be sequenced ahead of long-lead packages.

## Package structure logic

- **WP01–WP04** — Enabling works (investigation, land, earthworks, utilities diversion)
- **WP05–WP10** — Core logistics infrastructure (rail, yards, warehousing, cold chain)
- **WP11–WP14** — Buildings and border-facing facilities
- **WP15–WP17** — Digital and systems packages
- **WP18–WP20** — Commissioning, operations readiness and expansion provision

## Packaging notes for EPC procurement

1. **Single EPC vs. split packages.** A single EPC head contract (with nominated subcontracts for rail systems and digital) simplifies interface risk and suits a lender-friendly date-certain, price-certain wrap. Splitting WP15–WP17 into a separate systems integrator contract is common where the technology partner differs from the civil EPC.
2. **Rail packages (WP05–WP06)** require KTMB technical approval and possession planning; these are schedule-critical and should be early-engaged. *Any physical connection to the KTMB network is subject to agreements that are pending confirmation.*
3. **Long-lead items:** refrigeration plant (WP09), handling equipment (WP07), substation equipment (WP03), scanners/ANPR (WP17).
4. **Local content:** Malaysian regulatory and market practice favours meaningful local contractor participation (e.g., Bumiputera contractor programmes, CIDB registration requirements). Allocation to be structured during consortium formation.
