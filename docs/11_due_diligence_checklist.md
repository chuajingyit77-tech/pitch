# Section 11 — Due Diligence Checklist

> Purpose: the complete evidence set required before consortium formation, investment committee submissions and bank credit processes. Each item should be answered with documents, not statements.

## A. Corporate
1. Certificate of incorporation, constitution and statutory registers of the project owner and all related entities (SSM extracts)
2. Shareholding structure, ultimate beneficial owners, and any nominee arrangements
3. Board resolutions authorising the project and any agreements signed to date
4. Audited financial statements (3 years) of project owner entities
5. Existing debts, charges, guarantees (SSM/CTOS/bank searches)
6. Litigation, winding-up, tax arrears searches
7. Group structure chart and inter-company agreements
8. Any existing MOUs/agreements with government bodies, KTMB, contractors, investors — full copies with status

## B. Land
1. Land titles (geran/lease) for every parcel: registered owner, area, category of land use, express conditions and restrictions-in-interest
2. Whether land is state land, reserved land (e.g., railway reserve), Malay Reserve land, or private — and implications for foreign/corporate participation
3. Encumbrances: charges, caveats, easements, liens
4. Current zoning under the local plan (MPKangar/Perlis structure plan) and any conversion/rezoning requirements
5. Survey plans, boundary verification, encroachments
6. Acquisition/lease terms proposed: price, tenure, conditions precedent
7. Access rights to public roads and to the rail corridor (any KTMB/Railway Assets Corporation land involved?)
8. Flood history and drainage context; soil/geotechnical data if any exists

## C. Project
1. Any existing feasibility studies, master plans, traffic/cargo studies — full copies and authorship
2. Current status: what exactly exists today (approvals, designs, works) vs. what is aspiration
3. Project scope definition endorsed by the owner; land area and phasing intent
4. Any prior tenders, contractor engagements, or terminated agreements and their liabilities
5. Utilities availability studies: power capacity at nearest TNB intake, water, telecom
6. Interface status with Perlis Inland Port and existing Padang Besar terminal operators — cooperation, competition or integration?

## D. Government
1. Written evidence of state government support: MB office, EXCO decisions, state agencies (e.g., state economic planning unit)
2. Federal engagement status: MOT, MITI/MIDA, JKDM (Customs), Ministry of Finance/UKAS if PPP route
3. NCIA engagement status and any incentive discussions
4. KTMB/Railway Assets Corporation engagement on siding connection and services
5. Any gazettes, approvals or licences already issued (free zone, bonded, ICQS-related)
6. Thai-side engagement, if any (province, customs, SRT)
7. Political risk context: state/federal alignment, upcoming decision timelines

## E. Technical
1. Topographic and geotechnical data adequacy
2. Rail connection feasibility: gradient, curvature, possession constraints, KTMB technical standards
3. Road access capacity and JKR requirements
4. Utility loads vs. available capacity (especially cold chain power)
5. Flood risk modelling and mitigation cost implications
6. Environmental constraints: EIA screening category, buffer requirements
7. Constructability and logistics of construction at a border location

## F. Financial
1. Owner's funding capacity and commitments to date
2. Any existing financing arrangements, liens on project assets
3. Tax status and structure options (holding company location, incentives eligibility)
4. Insurance history and insurability review
5. Basis of any figures previously circulated by any party (validate or discard)

## G. Legal
1. Rights the owner actually holds versus rights claimed (land, mandates, approvals) — evidence for each
2. Foreign investment restrictions relevant to logistics, warehousing, forwarding licences
3. Licensing map: bonded warehouse (JKDM), forwarding agent, cold room (health), CIDB for contractors, CAAM for drones
4. Draft or executed agreements review: MOUs, JVs, EPC LOIs
5. Dispute history and any adverse claims to the site or project
6. Sanctions/compliance screening of all counterparties (including any Chinese SOE partners' listing status under relevant sanctions regimes)

## H. Commercial
1. Verified cargo baseline: rail and road volumes through Padang Besar (KTMB, Customs data requests)
2. Named prospective anchor customers and the status of any commitments
3. Competing/complementary facilities: Perlis Inland Port status and pipeline, Bukit Kayu Hitam developments, Thai-side facilities
4. Tariff benchmarks: current handling/storage pricing in the corridor
5. Demand-side interviews: forwarders, hauliers, shipping lines using the crossing

## I. Environmental & Social
1. EIA requirement determination (DOE) and expected conditions
2. Land use displacement: any occupants, farmers, businesses on site
3. Heritage/cultural constraints
4. ESG frameworks required by target lenders (IFC Performance Standards, Equator Principles)
5. Climate resilience: flood, heat; drainage design standards

## J. Operational
1. Proposed operating model: self-operate vs. operator partner; candidate operators
2. Labour availability and skills in Perlis; training needs
3. Border operating hours and process constraints (both sides) and how they cap throughput
4. Security requirements for bonded/border operations
5. Existing operator relationships at Padang Besar terminal (who does what today — verified)

## K. Digital
1. Existing systems (if any) at site/owner: anything to integrate or replace
2. Connectivity availability: fibre routes, carrier presence, latency to customs systems
3. JKDM system interface possibilities and constraints (declaration systems, gate release)
4. Data governance: PDPA, cross-border data flows with Thailand, cybersecurity obligations (Cyber Security Act 2024 designation risk)
5. Vendor landscape screening for TOS/WMS/ANPR suited to Malaysian border context
