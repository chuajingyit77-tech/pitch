# Section 8 — Proposed Role of Shandong Hi-Speed Group

> **Status:** Proposed scope for discussion. No agreement, mandate or commitment by Shandong Hi-Speed Group (SDHS) exists unless separately verified; corporate facts about SDHS are cited in Section 2 / References. Everything below is what SDHS **could** undertake, structured for negotiation.

## 8.1 Strategic fit (rationale, not commitment)

- SDHS is a large Chinese state-owned infrastructure group whose core competencies — expressways, rail-related infrastructure, logistics parks, and infrastructure investment — map directly onto this project's scope (see Section 2 for cited corporate facts).
- A Malaysia–Thailand border platform sits on the Kunming–Singapore / Pan-Asian rail corridor narrative relevant to Chinese outbound logistics strategy, subject to SDHS's own strategy confirmation.

## 8.3 Possible commercial postures for SDHS (options, to be negotiated)

1. **EPC Contractor only** — date-certain, price-certain EPC wrap; simplest entry; no equity.
2. **EPC + Equity investor** — SDHS invests in the SPV alongside its EPC role (common in Chinese outbound infrastructure); aligns delivery and ownership incentives; requires related-party safeguards for lenders.
3. **EPC + Equity + O&M participation** — deepest integration; SDHS shares long-term operating performance risk and upside.

## 8.4 What SDHS would need from the project (inputs for their internal approval)

- Verified land, rights and approvals status (Section 11 due diligence outputs)
- Bankable feasibility study and cargo demand study
- Clarity on concession/tenure structure and government support instruments
- Clear consortium governance and dispute resolution framework
- Compliance pathway: Malaysian foreign contractor licensing (CIDB), local content expectations, and Chinese outbound investment approvals (NDRC/MOFCOM/SAFE processes for the investment posture)

## 8.5 Interface principles

- Single EPC wrap preferred by lenders; if SDHS takes civil scope only, interface risk with rail systems and digital packages must be contractually closed (see Section 5 packaging notes).
- Technology stack (Section 6) to remain open-standard so Chinese, Malaysian and international vendors can compete at subsystem level.
