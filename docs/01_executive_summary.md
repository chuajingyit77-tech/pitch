# Section 1 — Executive Summary

## Why Padang Besar

Padang Besar is Malaysia's **only direct rail link with Thailand** and one of only two major road gateways on the Malaysia–Thailand border. Through it run: the KTMB electrified double-track corridor to Penang and Klang Valley; the CMA CGM/Infinity Penang landbridge block trains; the ASEAN Express rail service to Chongqing; and, since December 2025, daily freight into the new Perlis Inland Port. Thai customs data shows Padang Besar was **Thailand's fastest-growing major border checkpoint in 2024 (+25.9%, 102.3bn baht)**. The border's structural constraints — 06:00–22:00 road operating hours, legacy terminal saturation (TKPB at ~80% of 150,000 TEU capacity before migration), and single-track sections on the Thai side — are precisely what modern logistics infrastructure and digitalisation solve. (All figures: Section 2, with references.)

## Why Malaysia

Malaysia offers a stable, common-law investment environment with an explicit policy machine behind this corridor: the US$30bn Malaysia–Thailand trade target for 2027 (3rd JTC), the NCER/NCIA statutory development mandate for Perlis, the PIKAS 2030 PPP master plan, the 13MP road-to-rail agenda, and the July 2026 announcement of a Malaysia–Thailand **Border Economic Zone** spanning the northern states. Federal and state actors have already proven willingness to co-invest at this exact location (≈RM400m of NCIA external infrastructure; BPMB's RM374m PIP financing).

## Why border logistics

Land-border trade is ~40% of US$25bn bilateral trade and growing faster than the total. Border logistics is a chokepoint business: whoever owns the infrastructure where two customs regimes, two rail operators and two road networks meet captures structural rents from every improvement in flow — and cross-border flows are the least commoditised, highest-friction, highest-margin segment of ASEAN logistics. The China–ASEAN rail narrative (ASEAN Express; Kunming–Singapore corridor) adds a long-run growth option on top of the bilateral base.

## Why this project matters

- **National:** converts a congested crossing into a trade-facilitation asset supporting the US$30bn target and the road-to-rail policy.
- **State:** Perlis is Malaysia's smallest economy; the state government has made Padang Besar logistics its flagship growth bet.
- **Regional:** the corridor is the physical implementation of IMT-GT and ASEAN connectivity commitments.
- **Commercial:** verified demand growth + policy tailwind + an under-built border = a rare infrastructure entry point at the right moment in the cycle.

## Current opportunities

1. Ride verified cargo growth (border trade +25.9% at this checkpoint in 2024)
2. Cold chain at the border — no large-scale multi-user facility documented publicly (white space)
3. Truck marshalling/queue management against the 16-hour border window
4. Digital corridor services (booking, ANPR, clearance visibility) — no integrated system documented
5. VAS/light processing in free-zone conditions serving both Thai and Malaysian shippers
6. Partnership positioning with the operating Perlis Inland Port ecosystem (KTMB, Mutiara Perlis, Maersk MOC)

## Current challenges

1. **The incumbent question:** Perlis Inland Port is operating (600k TEU/yr capability, FCZ launched May 2026, state-backed). The project must define itself as integration, complement, or competitor — the single largest strategic decision, pending project-owner clarification.
2. Land, rights and mandate for this project are **unverified** — the package's gating item.
3. Border hours and Thai-side single-track constrain throughput until bilateral/SRT actions land.
4. Road cargo gravity is shifting to Bukit Kayu Hitam–Sadao (new link road opened July 2026).
5. Financing a greenfield border asset requires anchor cargo commitments and disciplined governance (the PKFZ lesson).

## Future opportunities

- SRT Hat Yai–Padang Besar double-tracking (bid-stage 2026) multiplies rail corridor capacity
- Border Economic Zone incentives (announced, details pending)
- Pan-Asian rail maturation (Thai standard-gauge phases to 2031) grows the China–ASEAN rail option
- Expansion phases: e-commerce fulfilment, halal hub aligned with CVIA, regional replication of the platform model at other crossings

## The honest bottom line

The corridor thesis is strong and largely verifiable. What is **not** yet verified is the project itself: its land, rights, mandate and relationship to the operating Perlis Inland Port. This package therefore does two things: (1) presents the verified opportunity to the standard investors expect, and (2) defines precisely the confirmations, due diligence and sequencing (Sections 11, 12, 15) required to convert the opportunity into an investable transaction. Nothing in this document assumes rights that have not been evidenced.
