Joint Research Proposal IIUM · Kulliyyah of Engineering · CUTe Draft for discussion

GroundTruth Air–ground collaborative recovery of oil palm loose fruit

Loose fruit carries roughly twice the oil of the bunch it falls from, and loses that value by the day it lies on the ground. Nobody has measured the clock. This programme measures it, and builds the machines that beat it.

01 The problem Why this is a value-recovery problem, not a labour problem

The most valuable fruit in the plantation is the fruit on the ground

A tenera bunch delivers a national average oil extraction rate of 19.86%. The loose fruitlets that detach from it carry over 40% oil by weight — the single richest material moving through the estate. Loose fruit accounts for 5–10% of total crop, and 3–7% of production is estimated lost in harvesting and in-field transport.

Collecting it is the lowest-paid, least-wanted job on the estate. Fruit-picking crews earn RM800–1,000 a month against a statutory minimum of RM1,700, and the national shortfall stands at roughly 40,000 workers, more than half of it concentrated in harvesting and in-field collection. Overall mechanisation of the crop sits near 11%.

Every serious attempt at this problem for the past century has been a mechanical one — suction rigs, rakes, hoppers. MPOB's own review is blunt: in a hundred years of commercial cultivation, the method has not fundamentally changed. The current generation of machines moves 1,200–1,500 kg a day but chokes on fronds, sinks in wet soil, and admits up to 15% debris. The most advanced robotic attempt — an arm-and-gripper picker built by one of the largest growers — recovers around 40 kg.

The gap between those two numbers is the whole thesis. Throughput comes from bulk collection; value comes from knowing which fruit is still worth collecting. No one is working on the second half.

02 The gap A measurable quantity that has never been measured in the field

The clock nobody has measured

Fresh, undamaged fruit yields oil at under 0.3% free fatty acid. The detachment scar is the entry point for lipase, which is why loose fruit is inherently more fragile than fruit still on the bunch. Bruise it and FFA at the wound climbs steeply within the hour; bruised fruit in contact with water reaches 10.44% FFA by day five, against a commercial ceiling normally set at 5%.

So loose fruit decays along two axes at once — oil is lost to germination and rot, and what oil remains is discounted for acidity. The economics of recovery therefore depend on a variable no one currently observes: how many days has this fruit been lying here?

The literature gap

Published FFA data comes almost entirely from laboratory bruising and immersion trials. We could not locate a single Malaysian field study that closes the chain from days on ground → FFA → RM per tonne. That curve does not exist. It is simultaneously an open research question, a pricing instrument, and a proprietary asset.

There is a second, sharper reason this matters. Graders already read ripeness from the number of fruitlets on the ground — the industry's oldest visual heuristic. Loose fruit is not merely a loss to be recovered; it is a signal about the block that produced it. Read it correctly and it tells you which blocks are over-ripe, which harvesting gangs are leaving crop behind, and where the next round should go.

03 Why air–ground Forced by the physiology of the crop, not chosen for novelty

The crop's own structure dictates the architecture

Oil palm defeats aerial sensing in a specific way: bunches sit beneath the canopy, and the ripest ones sit lowest. From directly above they are essentially invisible. This is why aerial ripeness assessment has stalled everywhere it has been attempted, and why MPOB's own aerial Bacillus thuringiensis trials against bagworm — 96% larval knockdown, yet pupae still recorded at 28 days — confirmed excellent upper-surface coverage and insufficient deposition underneath.

Both of the crop's highest-value problems are the same shape: invisible from the air, reachable from the ground. Meanwhile the ground robot has the opposite deficiency — it can see and touch anything within a few metres and knows nothing about the block it is standing in.

Ground-level loose fruit is the one high-value target that reverses the occlusion problem. It lies in the open, in the inter-row and around the palm circle, where a low-flying aircraft can see it and a canopy cannot hide it. It is the natural handshake between the two platforms.

Fig. 1 — The closed loop. The ground robot is not only the actuator; it is the annotator.

To our knowledge no published work combines UAV and UGV in oil palm. The components exist and are recent: insect-inspired online navigation for micro-aerial vehicles among oil palm specifically (2025); UAV-prior-guided UGV path planning demonstrated in banana plantations under closely comparable tropical, ground-constrained conditions (2026); UAV–UGV cooperative targeted spraying in pomelo orchards. Nobody has assembled them for this crop. That is the opening.

04 Work packages Three packages, three theses, one system

Programme structure

Each package stands alone as a publishable contribution and as a postgraduate project, while composing into a single field-validated system by month 18.

WP-A · Perception

Loose fruit detection and density mapping from low altitude

Detection of ground-level fruitlets under the conditions that defeat existing work: frond litter around the palm circle, partial burial in soft ground after rain, extreme illumination range between full sun and canopy shadow, and small, low-contrast targets against laterite. Existing published detection reaches 97% precision on curated ground imagery; none of it has been tested against these conditions, and no public Malaysian field dataset exists.

Output — The first open field dataset of ground-level oil palm loose fruit, with a detection benchmark. Published for the community; the extended proprietary corpus stays with the commercial partner.
WP-B · The clock

Dwell-time estimation and its economic calibration

Estimating how long a fruitlet has been on the ground from its visual signature — exocarp colour progression, germination, mud adhesion, fungal bloom — calibrated against FFA assayed on the same fruit at a partner mill laboratory. The output is not an accuracy figure but a curve: days on ground mapped to FFA, mapped to discount per tonne.

Output — The dwell-time → FFA → value relationship, established under Malaysian field conditions. Novel, citable, and directly usable as a commercial pricing basis.
WP-C · Coordination

Air-informed scheduling for time-critical recovery

Converting the aerial prior into ground-vehicle routing and sequencing under an objective that is neither distance nor coverage but recoverable oil value per unit time — which implies deliberately abandoning fruit whose remaining value no longer justifies the traverse. Closed-loop validation: predicted recoverable mass against mass actually delivered to the mill.

Output — A scheduling formulation with field validation, plus the closed-loop measurement protocol that makes outcome-based commercial contracts auditable.

Regulatory position

The programme flies sensing payloads, not dispensing payloads. Under CAD 6011 this places the work in the lightest applicable category — surveillance-class small unmanned aircraft under 20 kg, operated on standard authorisation — rather than requiring an Unmanned Aerial Work Certificate and agricultural pilot module, and it avoids the parallel Department of Agriculture approvals governing what may be dispensed from the air. This is a deliberate design choice: it is what allows field data collection to begin in weeks rather than quarters.

05 The partnership Complementary strengths, and one gap stated plainly

What each side brings

The Centre for Unmanned Technologies is the right partner for a specific reason: its published strength is computer and robotic vision, deep learning for robotics, and unmanned systems, demonstrated on autonomous surface vessels, driver-assistance, and vision-based inspection. WP-A and WP-C are that capability pointed at a new domain — not a request to develop competence the centre does not have.

One gap should be named at the outset. IIUM has no agriculture faculty, and CUTe has no published agronomic or aerial-application track record. WP-B requires FFA assay and agronomic sampling discipline that neither partner holds. The proposal is to close this with a third participant — a mill laboratory, which performs FFA titration as routine daily work — rather than to pretend the gap away. A mill partner also supplies the delivery-weight ground truth that WP-C's closed loop depends on, and is a natural first customer.

The channel argument

IIUM's distinguishing asset in this programme is not laboratory capacity. It is institutional reach: co-sponsored by eight member governments and OIC-affiliated since 2011, with over 6,000 international students drawn from more than 116 countries. Two of the world's five largest palm oil producers are OIC members, and Islamic Development Bank innovation instruments are open to member-state institutions. For technology intended to leave Malaysia, that is a channel no ranking captures — and it should be written into the collaboration's scope, not treated as incidental.

07 Principles Stated up front so the agreement drafts quickly

Intellectual property and data

Agricultural AI collaborations fail at the data clause, because standard research agreements do not have one. Four principles, offered as an opening position:

  • Background is inventoried. Pre-existing models, code, pipelines and airframe designs are listed in a schedule and transfer nothing by participating.
  • Data and weights are separated from publications. Raw field imagery, annotations and trained weights sit with the commercial partner; the university receives a perpetual non-commercial licence for teaching and research, and the benchmark subset is published openly under both names.
  • Foreground is licensed on terms fixed now. Rate, milestones and territory are agreed when the agreement is signed, not deferred. Territory is worldwide with sublicensing to affiliates and local partners, or the export thesis fails on contact.
  • Publication is encouraged, with a short review window. A 60-day pre-submission review, extendable only to file, applied consistently to papers and to student theses including repository deposit.

Grower data carries its own obligations: block-level yield and gang-performance data is commercially sensitive to the estate, and collection requires informed consent and personal-data compliance. Getting this right is a condition of estate access, not a formality.

What success looks like at month 12

One dataset nobody else has. One curve nobody else has published. One estate where predicted recoverable value was measured against delivered tonnage and the numbers agreed. Everything after that — additional estates, additional territories, additional crops that drop their fruit to the ground — is repetition of a proven measurement, not a new invention.
